\section*{Executive summary}
The certified reproduction of Liang \emph{et al.} (1997) yields a drain-refined spatial-convergence trio of the peak impact-ionization generation rate $G_{ii}$ at three meshes ($71\times58$, $100\times70$, $141\times85$ on a common frozen energy grid). This report documents the reproducibility campaign and its reviewer-approved corrected-methodology confirmation, and it incorporates an independent referee's ``major revisions'' feedback. Four findings:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The qualitative verdict is robust and reproduces everywhere tested}: the trio is significantly \emph{non-monotone} ($S_{\mathrm{rev}}\gg1$, a heuristic sign-reversal diagnostic, not a statistical score), so a conventional one-term Richardson extrapolation is not justified from these three points, and \textbf{there is no established continuum value of $G_{ii}$}.
\item \textbf{All three meshes certify to $<\!1\%$ under the same solver.} $71\times58$ $+0.006\%$ and $100\times70$ $+0.40\%$ are from the current corrected-methodology run (git \texttt{4e2374e}); $141\times85$ $+0.79\%$ is from an \emph{earlier} run (git \texttt{1893b9c}) whose only source difference is a monitoring script (zero physics/numerics), hence solver-equivalent. The large mid-investigation $\sim\!10$--$12\%$ platform differences were finite-iteration (premature-stop) artifacts, not converged discrepancies.
\item \textbf{The reproducible quantity is the physical observable, not the outer residual.} A diagnostic continuation (each converged state continued $100$ iterations with stopping \emph{disabled}) shows the observables ($G_{ii}$, $\int G_{ii}\,dV$, drain current) are flat to $<\!0.35\%$, while the outer residual $R_\phi$ is itself ill-conditioned and \emph{does not stay} below the $10^{-4}$ tolerance (it oscillates in a mesh-dependent band mostly \emph{above} tolerance). A single $R_\phi$ crossing is therefore not a sufficient certification; observable flatness is. This confirms the referee's central methodological point and strengthens the reproducibility conclusion.
\item \textbf{Convergence difficulty sharpens with mesh refinement.} Iterations-to-$R_\phi$ rose $325\to929$ ($71\times58\to100\times70$); the $R_\phi$ noise floor rose correspondingly ($\sim\!1.8\ten{-4}\to\sim\!4.5\ten{-4}$); and on the finest mesh $141\times85$ the current run's \emph{inner} (Krylov/ILU) linear solve stagnated and the outer iteration hung (classified \emph{unresolved} for that run). The finest mesh is the fragile one: one run hung, an earlier run certified at $+0.79\%$.
\end{enumerate}

Peak $G_{ii}$ is a near-null-space, high-energy-tail functional, hypersensitive to the platform floating-point path. A staged frozen-state probe (Appendix C) localizes the \emph{first} cross-platform divergence to sparse-system assembly ($\sim\!10^{-16}$, single-threaded), upstream of the solve. This rounding-scale seed perturbs the convergence \emph{path, rate, and residual}, but not the converged observable. The recommended methodology is to certify on sustained observable flatness (and report a well-conditioned integrated observable and a properly-defined terminal current), rather than on a single outer-residual crossing or the over-strict pointwise plateau gate.

\section{System, reference, and method}
First-order spherical-harmonic expansion (SHE) of the electron Boltzmann equation in total-energy form, self-consistent with 2D Poisson and hole continuity; nonparabolic Kane band; DOS-weighted acoustic + optical-phonon + Keldysh-surrogate impact-ionization operators. The SHE linear system is solved with LGMRES preconditioned by a physics-split incomplete-LU (\texttt{scipy.sparse.linalg.spilu}); the outer map is Anderson-accelerated ($\text{depth}=6$, $\beta=0.5$). The outer fixed-point is stopped on the potential residual $R_\phi=\max|G(\phi)-\phi|$ over free DOFs (the raw fixed-point residual, not the damped Anderson step), tolerance $\mathrm{tol}_\phi=10^{-4}$. A legacy acceptance gate (last-4 cumulative-iteration spread of $G_{ii,\max}$ and $G_{tot}\le3\ten{-4}$ with $R_\phi\le1.5\ten{-3}$) is retained as a diagnostic only; \S\ref{sec:diag} shows both $R_\phi$ and that gate are inadequate as sole certification criteria.

\textbf{Certified reference (Sep-24, Windows, pure-Python kernel, git \texttt{d30b471}, equivalent to the frozen tag):}
\[
G_{ii}=3.075619/2.136180/2.387429\ten{25}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}s^{-1}},\quad S_{\mathrm{rev}}=737,\ \text{non-monotone.}
\]
Common frozen $H$-grid hash \texttt{b4319bb03303} (591 energy points) across all meshes and runs. This reference was \emph{plateau}-certified under the legacy gate; its per-node outer residual is not uniformly $<10^{-4}$ (see \S\ref{sec:diag}), which is addressed by the observable-based criterion below.

\textbf{The $100\times70$ node is the decisive in-run demonstration} of the premature-stop mechanism. Its history: a flat plateau near $1.90\ten{25}$ (with $G_{ii}$ drifting slightly \emph{downward}, $1.96\to1.90$) and $R_\phi$ oscillating in the $5\ten{-4}$--$2.4\ten{-3}$ band from iter $\sim\!50$ to $\sim\!650$; then a climb over iters $\sim\!700$--$894$ to the reference, certifying at 929 ($R_\phi\,7.0\ten{-5}$). A fixed-iteration harness stopping at $\sim\!360$ (as the original trio harness did) would have recorded $\sim\!1.90\ten{25}$ ($-11\%$) and mis-read the down-drift as divergence --- precisely the artifact that produced the withdrawn $-12\%$.

\textbf{The current run's own $141\times85$ did not certify.} Warm-chained from the current certified $100\times70$, it advanced to outer iteration 281 and then \textbf{hung}: the process remained compute-bound (6.6 cores, 47 threads) for $\sim\!11.5$\,h on a single outer step while the log stayed frozen --- i.e.\ the \emph{inner} LGMRES/ILU linear solve stagnated (cycling without returning). It was killed and is classified \textbf{unresolved} for the current run. This is a genuine finite-mesh robustness failure, not a plateau: on $100\times70$ the iteration kept advancing; on $141\times85$ it stopped advancing entirely. The earlier-run certification ($+0.79\%$) shows the finest mesh \emph{can} converge under the same solver, but it is slow and fragile --- the near-null-space conditioning taken to its limit.

\textbf{Reading.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{A single $R_\phi$ crossing is not sustained convergence (referee confirmed), on both platforms.} On $71\times58$, $90/100$ (Windows) and $82/100$ (Linux) continued iterations sit \emph{above} $10^{-4}$; the stop had caught a transient dip. On $100\times70$, the Windows reference state recomputes to $R_\phi=2.57\ten{-4}$ --- it never satisfies $10^{-4}$ at all ($100/100$ above) --- consistent with its plateau-certification rather than an $R_\phi<10^{-4}$ certification.
\item \textbf{But the observables are converged.} $G_{ii}$, $G_{tot}$, and drain current are flat to $<\!0.35\%$ across all $100$ iterations on both meshes. The state is parked at the converged solution; only the residual jitters.
\item \textbf{Decisive cross-platform datum.} Linux $100\times70$ stopped at $R_\phi=6.97\ten{-5}$ (a dip), the Windows reference parks at $R_\phi=2.57\ten{-4}$: the residuals differ by $\sim\!4\times$, yet the observables agree to $0.40\%$. The observable is the reproducible converged quantity; $R_\phi$ is an ill-conditioned metric.
\item \textbf{The residual noise floor rises with refinement} (median $1.8\ten{-4}\to4.5\ten{-4}$ from $71\times58$ to $100\times70$, and inner-solve stagnation on $141\times85$) --- residual-level evidence for the conditioning sharpening with mesh density.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Consequence for certification:} certify on sustained observable flatness (e.g.\ $G_{ii}$/$G_{tot}$ drift over a fixed window), not on a single $R_\phi$ crossing nor the over-strict pointwise plateau gate.

\section{Mechanism}
Peak $G_{ii}$ is numerically ill-conditioned: (i) a tail-dominated functional ($\varepsilon\gtrsim1.5$\,eV) controlled by a tiny near-null-space of the solution (a $10^{-8}$ linear residual does not pin it); (ii) floating-point-path-dependent assembly and solves (Appendix C: assembly coefficients differ across OS at $\sim\!10^{-16}$, single-threaded, from vectorized \texttt{exp}/\texttt{pow} in the coefficient kernels); (iii) amplification over hundreds of outer iterations. These compound along the ill-conditioned direction, producing large \emph{finite-iteration} deviations, a noisy outer residual (\S\ref{sec:diag}), slow and variable convergence (Finding 4), and on the finest mesh an inner-solve stagnation --- but \emph{not} a non-reproducible converged observable.

\section{Remediation (proposed; none applied to the frozen reference)}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Certify on the observable, not the residual.} Require sustained flatness of $G_{ii}$ (and $\int G_{ii}\,dV$) over a fixed window; report $R_\phi$ alongside as a diagnostic with its (mesh-dependent) noise floor. A single $R_\phi$ crossing is insufficient (\S\ref{sec:diag}).
\item \textbf{Run each mesh to its own convergence}, not a common iteration budget; the finest meshes need a larger budget (and may still stagnate --- see direct solve).
\item \textbf{Direct linear solve} where memory permits (raise the direct-solve DOF cutoff), removing the Krylov/ILU floating-point-path dependence and the inner-solve stagnation seen on $141\times85$. \emph{This is a method change and belongs in a separate error-budget study, not the frozen-method trio.}
\item \textbf{Report a well-conditioned integrated observable and a properly-defined terminal current.} Quote $\int G_{ii}\,dV$ (generation rate per out-of-plane width) and the substrate hole current at the body contact including recombination (audit F-08); note $\int G_{ii}\,dV$ is \emph{not} itself the collected terminal current. The diagnostic already records the drain current ($\sim\!3.0\ten{-4}$ A/$\mu$m, drift $<\!0.1\%$) and relative number balance ($\sim\!10^{-4}$); the spatial convergence of $\int G_{ii}\,dV$ is \emph{not} yet demonstrated (node-to-node $G_{tot}$ changes $\sim\!35\%$, a discretization effect distinct from floating-point conditioning).
\end{itemize}

\section{Status and scope}\label{sec:status}
The earlier $\sim\!12\%$ platform conclusion is \textbf{withdrawn}. \textbf{Supported:} the non-monotone trio; slow, path-dependent convergence with difficulty rising on finer meshes (iteration count, residual floor, and finally inner-solve stagnation); cross-platform agreement of the \emph{observable} to $<\!0.5\%$ on the two meshes tested this way; all three meshes certifying to $<\!1\%$ under the same solver. \textbf{Not established:} a clean single-run three-mesh trio (node~3 is an earlier solver-equivalent run; the current $141\times85$ is unresolved); a platform-dependent converged solution; pointwise $G_{ii}$-plateau or single-$R_\phi$-crossing convergence; spatial (continuum) convergence of $G_{ii}$ or $\int G_{ii}\,dV$; quantitative equivalence to the original 1997 device physics (the solver is a reconstructed/surrogate model).

\textbf{Three distinct questions} should not be conflated: \emph{numerical reproducibility} (same equations $\to$ similar observables across platforms under matched stopping --- substantially supported on two meshes), \emph{discretization convergence} (grids fine enough for a continuum claim --- not established; anisotropic $x$-only/$y$-only refinement needed), and \emph{physical validity} (correct device physics and terminal currents vs Liang 1997 or experiment --- a separate study).

\textbf{Report two quantities.} Quote $G_{ii}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ (at observable convergence) with the window variability $\Delta G_{ii}^{\mathrm{tail}}=(\max-\min)/\langle\cdot\rangle$ and the $R_\phi$ noise band, so the outer residual is not misread as proof of pointwise convergence.

\section*{Appendix B: Direct platform-FP probe (one-shot)}
The identical $71\times58$ cold solve, run 3 capped outer iterations on both OSs with bit-identical mesh/energy-grid input and the \emph{same} OpenBLAS 0.3.33: $\phi$ diverges at $2.7\ten{-11}$ (well-conditioned) while $G_{ii}$ diverges at $6.6\ten{-4}$ pointwise (near-null-space), growing $5.7\ten{-13}\to2.2\ten{-12}\to1.5\ten{-10}$ over 3 iters. Establishes sensitivity/amplification; Appendix C localizes the first-divergence stage.

\section*{Appendix C: Staged localization and the reversing ensemble}
A frozen-state probe (\texttt{audit/fixed\_state\_probe.py}) froze one $71\times58$ state, copied identical bytes to both OSs, and assembled/solved single-threaded: state, CSR structure, $H$, active map all bit-identical; $A.\mathtt{data}$ and $b$ differ at $\sim\!1.1\ten{-16}$ (last ULP); a single solve reproduces $G_{ii}^{\mathrm{peak}}$ across OS to $3.8\ten{-13}$ (so SuperLU/LGMRES is not the primary seed). First divergence is thus in assembly-coefficient floating point (platform NumPy/libm elementary functions), upstream of the solve. A perturbation ensemble (control + three $10^{-16}$ seeds) initially spanned $\sim\!10\%$ at 400 iters (seed 3 at $-11\%$), then \emph{merged} when continued to the $R_\phi$ tolerance at 422--613 iters: all four within $0.66\%$ of the Windows reference (seed 3 climbed to $+0.03\%$). This established the $-12\%$ as a premature-stop artifact; the $100\times70$ production node (\S\ref{sec:confirm}) independently reproduced the late break on a second mesh. A driver-equivalence experiment (Windows, continuous vs Anderson-reset-every-60) differs by only $0.44\%$, rejecting Anderson reset as the dominant driver effect; the driver sensitivity is a symptom of under-convergence on the ill-conditioned problem.
